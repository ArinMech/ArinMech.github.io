Automated Chess Board

A board that moves its own pieces, so you can play a real game without a real opponent. The interesting engineering wasn't the mechanism — it was getting the whole thing to a design a factory could quote at a price people would actually pay.

Role
Team Lead
Target price
$700 sell price
Process
Plastic injection molding
Tools
Fusion 360 · aPriori

Problem

  • Playing chess normally requires an opponent in the room; existing automated boards on the market are expensive.
  • An automated board is only worth designing if it can be manufactured at a price that undercuts what's already for sale.
  • That makes cost a design constraint from the first sketch, not an afterthought.

Approach

  • Modeled the full board and produced engineering drawings in Fusion 360.
  • Designed an internal 2-axis system to move pieces, specifying off-the-shelf components wherever a custom part wasn't justified.
  • Ran a DFM review and selected plastic injection molding for the board, corner mounts, slider mounts, and electromagnet holder.
  • Cost-analyzed the design in aPriori against the actual CAD geometry, not a spreadsheet estimate.

Validation

  • Checked every molded part against draft, wall thickness, and tooling feasibility before locking the design.
  • Used the aPriori results to test whether the target price survived contact with real process costs.

Outcome

  • Landed on a $700 target sell price — substantially below comparable products.
  • Four parts specified for injection molding, with the rest sourced off the shelf.
  • End-to-end demonstration of mechanism design, DFM, and cost-driven CAD.
