Regulated Panel Sizing

SAE requires structural proof for every regulated panel on the car. Rather than iterate by hand, I wrote a solver that swept the laminate design space, shortlisted the two strongest candidates, and then made them earn it on a load frame.

Role
Project Lead
Scope
Sizing, simulation, layup, physical test
Materials
T700 / T800 CF, aluminum core
Deliverable
Structures Equivalency Spreadsheet

Problem

  • Every structural panel on an SAE car has to be proven equivalent to a regulated baseline before the car is allowed to compete.
  • The laminate design space is enormous — ply count, orientation, material, and core thickness all trade against each other, and weight matters everywhere.
  • Hand-iterating a handful of layups risks leaving both stiffness and mass on the table.

Approach

  • Analyzed panels from first principles using solid mechanics and Composite Laminate Theory.
  • Wrote a ply-sweeper in Python that iterated 20,000+ ply schedules and ranked them on stiffness and energy absorption.
  • Simulated the shortlisted panels in Ansys — Static Structural plus ACP for the composite definition.
  • Modeled panel locations on the chassis in Creo Parametric to keep the analysis tied to real geometry.

Validation

  • Manufactured the two shortlisted panels by hand layup — T700/T800 carbon fiber skins over an aluminum core.
  • Ran 3-point bend and perimeter shear tests to real stress–strain curves.
  • Compared measured response against the simulated prediction rather than trusting the model alone.

Outcome

  • Total regulated panel weight minimized to ~22 lbs while staying compliant.
  • Completed the Structures Equivalency Spreadsheet (SES) and submitted it to SAE for review.
  • Left the team with a reusable sizing tool instead of a one-off answer.
